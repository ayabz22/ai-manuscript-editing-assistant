# Citation & Evidence Verification Review

## Scientific Assessment

The sentence bundles two distinct claims under one citation pair:

1. Protein C has anti-inflammatory properties.
2. Reduced protein C is associated with organ dysfunction and mortality in severe sepsis.

Only the second claim is what **Yan et al. (2001)** and **Shaw et al. (2011)** actually tested.

---

## Evidence Review

### Reference 1

**Yan et al. (Chest, 2001)**  
*Low Levels of Protein C Are Associated With Poor Outcome in Severe Sepsis.*

Retrospective analysis (subset of the ISS trial, *n* = 70) evaluating whether Protein C levels predict 30-day mortality, shock, ICU duration, and ventilator dependence.

**Supports**

- Association between reduced Protein C and mortality.
- Association between reduced Protein C and organ dysfunction.

**Does Not Support**

- Characterizing Protein C primarily as an anti-inflammatory mediator.

The study frames Protein C deficiency as a marker of coagulopathy and poor prognosis rather than investigating anti-inflammatory mechanisms.

---

### Reference 2

**Shaw et al. (Journal of Critical Care, 2011)**  
*Severe protein C deficiency is associated with organ dysfunction in patients with severe sepsis.*

Analysis of the PROWESS placebo cohort (*n* = 775). Severe Protein C deficiency was associated with pulmonary, renal, and hematologic organ dysfunction, as well as increased 28-day mortality.

**Supports**

- Association between reduced Protein C and organ dysfunction.
- Association between reduced Protein C and mortality.

**Does Not Support**

- Characterizing Protein C primarily as an anti-inflammatory mediator.

Like Yan et al., this study evaluates clinical outcomes rather than biological mechanisms.

---

## Recommended Revision

Split the citation so each clause is supported by literature that directly addresses that specific claim.

> *Protein C, an anti-inflammatory mediator,³ is reduced in severe sepsis, and lower levels are associated with organ dysfunction and mortality.¹˒²*

Where **Reference 3** is a mechanistic paper describing activated Protein C signaling and its anti-inflammatory properties (e.g., Esmon's work).

---

## Rationale

Citing Yan et al. and Shaw et al. for the phrase **"anti-inflammatory mediator"** implies that these studies established or investigated an anti-inflammatory mechanism.

They did not.

Both papers support the association between Protein C deficiency and adverse clinical outcomes but do not specifically support the mechanistic characterization presented in the manuscript.

The citations themselves are appropriate.

The issue is that the manuscript attributes a biological interpretation that extends beyond the emphasis of the cited studies.

Revising the wording—or citing a dedicated mechanistic reference—would improve scientific precision while preserving the manuscript's overall conclusion.
